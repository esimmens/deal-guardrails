# Oriel Speech: Non-Standard Deal Approval Policy, v4.2.1

Effective: 1 August 2026
Owner: Deal Desk
Supersedes: v4.2 (August 2026)
Applies to: every negotiated contract for text to speech, speech to text and conversational agent minutes
Machine-readable form: `policy/pricing_policy.yaml`. The approval system evaluates that file. This document is the reference text and cites the same rule identifiers in square brackets.

## §1 Purpose and scope

1.1 This policy states who must approve a negotiated contract whose commercial or legal terms differ from Oriel Speech's standard terms, and how a request for that approval is made. Its purpose is that every non-standard deal is approved by the people who carry the consequences of the term in question, that each approval is recorded before the customer signs, and that a requester can predict the answer before asking.

1.2 It applies to every contract negotiated by an Account Executive on behalf of Oriel Speech: new business, renewals, expansions and amendments, across all product lines and all segments (small business, mid-market, enterprise and public sector). It does not apply to self-serve subscriptions bought at published prices, which carry no negotiated terms. The published annual billing allowance on self-serve tiers is a list price, not a discount under this policy.

1.3 The fiscal year begins in January. Thresholds expressed in dollars are in United States dollars and apply to the net total of the contract (§2), not to the annual figure, unless a section says otherwise.

1.4 Requirements under this policy accumulate. A term that requires Finance approval under §4 still requires it when the discount is within Account Executive authority under §3, and nothing in one section removes an approval required by another. Where this document and the machine-readable policy are found to differ, Deal Desk decides which is in error and corrects the other; until it does, the request is treated as requiring Deal Desk.

1.5 This policy binds Account Executives, Deal Desk, Finance, Legal, Product and the Chief Revenue Officer. A person acting in more than one of those roles is bound by §8.

## §2 Definitions

**List price.** The price for a product, plan or unit as published on the Oriel Speech pricing page or, for enterprise units with no published price (for example committed agent minutes above the published tiers), the rate on the enterprise rate card that Deal Desk maintains for the current fiscal year. Where a quote mixes published and rate-card units, the list price of the contract is the sum of the list prices of its lines at the quoted volumes.

**Discount.** The percentage by which the net total of a contract is below its list price, measured across the whole contract and the whole term, computed to the hundredth of a percent. A discount is a single number for the contract: a ramp that is deep in year one and at list in year three is measured over all three years, and a waived fee is treated as a discount on the line it waives. The discount is never measured on one line or one year in isolation.

**Net total.** The sum of everything the customer is contractually committed to pay over the term after all discounts, credits, waivers and ramps, excluding taxes and excluding usage the customer may buy but has not committed to. Also called the net total contract value.

**Annual contract value.** The net total divided by the term in years. It is reported for information. The thresholds in this policy are set on the net total and on the discount, not on the annual figure.

**Competitive deal.** A deal in which the customer has stated, or the Account Executive has documented from the customer's own material, that a named competing vendor is under active evaluation for the same use case. A general sense that the market is competitive is not a competitive deal. The competitor and the evidence are recorded in the request.

**Renewal.** A contract that continues a relationship with a customer whose existing negotiated contract is ending, whether or not the renewed scope, volume or price is the same. An expansion signed mid-term is an amendment, not a renewal, unless it also extends the end date of the existing contract.

**Non-standard legal terms.** Any departure from the current Oriel Speech master subscription agreement, data processing addendum or order form, including: a change to the indemnity cap or its scope; a custom data residency commitment (processing or storage pinned to a named region beyond the published options); a data processing agreement supplied by the customer in place of ours; or a custom master services agreement, including the customer's own paper. Negotiated commercial fields on our own order form (price, volume, term, payment terms) are not legal terms.

**Termination for convenience.** A right for the customer to end the contract before the end of its term without cause, whether or not a notice period or a termination fee applies. A right to terminate for uncured material breach is standard and is not termination for convenience.

**Outcome-based pricing.** Any structure in which fees, or part of the fees, are tied to a customer outcome or a usage milestone rather than to a fixed commitment: for example a rate that steps down once the customer's agent containment rate passes a threshold, fees contingent on a go-live date, or a commitment that is only owed once monthly minutes exceed a stated level. Ordinary usage overage at a fixed per-unit rate is not outcome-based pricing.

**Implementation arrangements.** Professional services, onboarding or integration work performed by Oriel Speech or a partner that is bundled into the commercial terms, whether priced, waived, credited or exchanged for something else (for example a logo right or a reference call). Standard self-serve onboarding documentation is not an implementation arrangement.

**License fee restructuring.** Any change to the fixed-fee components of a contract: changing platform or seat fees, introducing or altering a ramp, or moving fees between line items (for example folding a platform fee into per-minute rates, or the reverse). Restructuring is a term in its own right even when the net total is unchanged, because it changes how and when revenue is recognised.

**Strategic account.** A designation that Deal Desk assigns to a named customer for a fiscal year, recorded in the account record, on criteria Deal Desk publishes. A requester may claim that an account is strategic, but the claim does not confer the designation, and the approval system treats an unverified claim as a claim, not a fact (§6).

## §3 Discount authority

### §3.1 Standard bands

3.1.1 Discounts of up to and including 15 percent¹ are within Account Executive authority and do not require a request under this section [R1a]. The Account Executive records the discount on the quote. This is an authority to quote, not an authority to approve a request (§8).

3.1.2 Discounts above 15 percent and up to 30 percent require Deal Desk approval [R1b].

3.1.3 Discounts above 30 percent require the Chief Revenue Officer, with Deal Desk review in addition [R1c]. Deal Desk reviews the structure and records its recommendation on the request before it reaches the Chief Revenue Officer.

3.1.4 Bands are inclusive of their upper bound: a discount of exactly 15 percent is within Account Executive authority and a discount of 15.01 percent is not. The discount is the whole-contract figure defined in §2. A request that reports a year-one or single-line figure instead is returned to the requester.

3.1.5 A discount within a band never removes an approval required by §4 to §7. The bands say who approves the price. They say nothing about who approves the terms.

### §3.2 Multi-year commitments

3.2.1 Customers committing to 24 months or more, paid annually or fully in advance, have historically been granted additional room beyond the standard band in recognition of the cash-flow benefit. Deal Desk maintains the schedule of allowances [R2].

3.2.2 A request for such a deal at a discount above 15 percent is submitted under §9 in the usual way and states the term and the payment terms accurately, so that Deal Desk can apply the schedule.

### §3.3 Competitive deals and renewals

3.3.1 Every request states whether the deal is a renewal and whether it is competitive, as defined in §2. Neither status by itself requires an approval or adds an approver; Deal Desk takes both into account when a request already requires its approval under §3.1. A competitive claim is supported by the evidence §2 describes. Misstating either fact is a breach of this policy and voids any approval obtained on the strength of it.

### §3.4 Other price concessions

3.4.1 Free months, service credits, waived fees and rate holds within the term are discounts and are included in the whole-contract figure. A price commitment that outlasts the term (a fixed renewal rate, or a promise to match the lowest price offered to any other customer) is a departure from the standard order form and is a non-standard legal term under §5 as well as a discount.

## §4 Payment terms

4.1 Standard payment terms are net-30 from invoice¹. Annual payment in advance, and full payment in advance for a multi-year term, are also standard and require no approval under this section. Their effect on discount is dealt with in §3.2.

4.2 Net-45, net-60, net-90 or any other schedule (for example quarterly in arrears, milestone billing, or payment on the customer's acceptance) requires Finance approval [R3]. "Other" is read broadly: any arrangement that is not one of the named standard terms is non-standard, however the customer describes it.

4.3 Payment terms are assessed on the whole contract. A contract that is net-30 in year one and net-60 thereafter is a net-60 contract for this section.

4.4 Finance may attach conditions to its approval (a deposit, a credit review, a shorter term) and those conditions become terms of the request. A request that later drops a condition is a new request (§9.6).

## §5 Legal and contract terms

5.1 Non-standard legal terms, as defined in §2, require Legal approval [R4]. This includes any change to the indemnity cap, any custom data residency commitment, a customer-supplied data processing agreement, and a custom or customer-paper master agreement, however small the redline appears.

5.2 Termination for convenience requires Legal approval and Deal Desk review [R5]. Legal approves the exposure. Deal Desk reviews the effect on the commitment: a contract the customer can end at will is, for revenue and forecasting purposes, a contract only for the length of the notice period, and the discount and payment terms are assessed on that basis. Both are required even when the termination right carries a fee.

5.3 Outcome-based pricing requires Product and Finance approval [R6]. Product approves because the outcome must map to a unit Oriel Speech can measure and bill, and the measurement has to be one the platform can produce. Finance approves because contingent fees change when revenue is recognised and how the commitment is forecast.

5.4 An implementation arrangement requires Product approval [R7], because bundled services commit delivery capacity that Product schedules, whether or not the services are priced.

5.5 License fee restructuring requires Finance approval [R8], because moving fees between line items, adding a ramp, or changing platform or seat fees changes revenue recognition even when the net total does not change.

5.6 Legal's approval is of the wording. It does not approve the price, and a Legal approval never substitutes for an approval required under §3, §4 or §7. The same holds in reverse: an approval of the price does not approve a redline.

## §6 Strategic accounts

6.1 Deal Desk may designate an account as strategic for the fiscal year, on published criteria (for example a named account in the annual plan, a reference customer in a target sector, or a customer whose usage growth is material to the plan). The designation is recorded in the account record with the date and the Deal Desk member who made it.

6.2 For a designated strategic account, Deal Desk may sponsor an exception to the standard bands in §3 or the standard terms in §4: it may recommend to the approvers this policy names that a term outside the standard position be granted, and it may prepare the request on the Account Executive's behalf. The designation changes what Deal Desk is prepared to recommend. It does not change who must approve: an exception for a strategic account is approved by exactly the roles the underlying term requires under §3 to §7, and §8 applies to it in full.

6.3 A requester may state in a request that the account is strategic. The statement is recorded as an unverified claim and has no effect on the approvals required [R9]. Deal Desk checks the claim against the account record. If the designation exists, Deal Desk may act under 6.2; if it does not, the claim is noted and the request proceeds on its other facts. A claim never reduces a requirement, and a request that relies on an unverified claim to justify a term is returned.

## §7 Deal size and sector

7.1 A contract whose net total is one million dollars or more¹ requires the Chief Revenue Officer, in addition to any approvals required by §3 to §6 [R11]. The threshold is on the net total defined in §2: after discount, over the whole term, excluding uncommitted usage.

7.2 A deal in the public-sector segment (government at any level, public agencies, public education and public health bodies, and prime contractors buying on their behalf) requires Legal approval, because public procurement terms displace the standard contract: statutory clauses, records and audit obligations, and the customer's own purchasing conditions apply whether or not they appear in the redline [R12]. This applies even where the customer signs Oriel Speech's paper without change.

7.3 The Chief Revenue Officer's approval under 7.1 is not a general waiver. It is approval of the deal as described in the request, on the terms the other required approvers have approved.

## §8 Self-approval and conflicts

8.1 An Account Executive may never approve their own request, at any level and for any term. The authority in §3.1 is an authority to quote within a band. Once a request exists, the person who submitted it is a requester and not an approver for that request, whatever other role they hold.

8.2 If the only holder of a required role is the requester (for example a member of Deal Desk submits a request for an account they manage, or the Chief Revenue Officer submits a request directly), the request escalates to that person's manager, who approves in the role or names someone else in the role's reporting line to do so. The escalation is recorded on the request.

8.3 Approvals are recorded by the approver, in the approval system, against the request. A requester may never record an approval on an approver's behalf, and a request that asserts an approval is treated as unapproved until the approver's own record exists.

8.4 A verbal yes on a call is not an approval. Neither is a message in a channel, a forwarded email, or a comment in the deal record. Any of these may be attached to the request as context, and the approver is asked to record their decision.

8.5 An approver who has a personal or financial interest in the customer, or who reports to the requester, declares it and recuses. 8.2 then applies.

## §9 How to submit a request

9.1 Requests are made through the intake assistant in the deal-desk channel. The assistant collects the following fields, in this order, and does not submit a request until each has an answer:

1. Account name.
2. Contract amount, and what the amount is: list total, net total, annual value, or per-unit price times volume. The assistant asks for whatever is missing if the amount given does not allow both the net total and the list price to be established.
3. Discount percentage, measured as §2 defines it.
4. Term in months.
5. Payment terms: net-30, net-45, net-60, net-90, annual prepaid, multi-year prepaid, or other.
6. Eight yes/no questions, in this order: termination for convenience; non-standard legal terms; outcome-based pricing; implementation arrangement; license fee restructuring; strategic-account claim; renewal; competitive.
7. Segment: small business, mid-market, enterprise, or public sector.

9.2 The assistant computes nothing about authority itself. It reads what the requester wrote, fills the fields above, and passes them to the policy engine, which evaluates the machine-readable policy and names the required approvers and the rules that require them. The engine's output is the receipt. It shows what the requester wrote, what the assistant read from it, which rules fired and which approvals are required, together with the version of the policy that was applied. The receipt is posted to the request thread and is the reference for any dispute about what was submitted.

9.3 Where the policy is silent on a case, or two of its provisions bear on the same facts without saying which governs, the engine does not choose. The request is routed to Deal Desk, and the receipt names the rule that could not be resolved, so that Deal Desk decides it on the record.

9.4 In the approval system the roles in this policy carry the following keys: ae (Account Executive), deal_desk (Deal Desk), finance (Finance), legal (Legal), product (Product) and cro (Chief Revenue Officer). The ae key identifies the requester's own authority and is never a reviewer.

9.5 If the assistant reads a term as present in the requester's description and the requester answers no to the corresponding question, the term is treated as present and Deal Desk resolves the difference. The assistant may add a reviewer it considers necessary; it may never remove one that the engine has required. No approval is ever attributed to the assistant, the engine or any other automated system.

9.6 A request is for a deal as described. If any field in 9.1 changes after submission, the requester submits a new request and the earlier approvals lapse. Approvals are recorded against the request, not against the account, and do not carry over to a later deal with the same customer.

9.7 A quote is released to the customer only when every approval the receipt names has been recorded under §8.3.

## §10 Change history

- September 2026, v4.2.1: §3.3.1 clarified after evaluation of the intake assistant showed it reading the sentence as a routing rule. Renewal and competitive status inform Deal Desk's review and never by themselves require an approval. No rule identifiers or thresholds changed.
- August 2026, v4.2: §2 definitions expanded to cover outcome-based pricing, implementation arrangements and license fee restructuring, with the corresponding approvals at §5.3 to §5.5 [R6, R7, R8]. §7.2 public-sector routing added [R12]. §9 rewritten for the intake assistant: fixed field order, the receipt, and the role keys. §8.2 extended to requesters in any role.
- March 2026 memo (M-2026-03): Deal Desk ceiling raised from 25 percent to 30 percent for competitive renewals only [R10].
- January 2026, v4.1: fiscal-year restatement. §4 rewritten: net-30 confirmed as standard; annual and multi-year payment in advance listed as standard. §8.3 and §8.4 added: approvals are recorded by the approver, and a verbal yes is not an approval.
- August 2025, v4.0: policy restructured into numbered sections. Strategic-account designation moved to Deal Desk (§6). Multi-year allowance schedule moved out of the policy text and into Deal Desk's keeping (§3.2).
- Earlier versions: superseded; available from Deal Desk on request.
