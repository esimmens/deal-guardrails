# How the Oriel Speech policy was derived

Oriel Speech is fictional. Its deal approval policy (`policy/deal_approval_policy.md`, machine form `policy/pricing_policy.yaml`) was not invented from nothing. The *shape* of the policy (bands by discount percentage, approvers stacked by term type, no self-approval, approvals that lapse) comes from published SaaS discount-governance and deal-desk practice. The *vocabulary* (the term types, the approver parties, the price ladder being discounted from) comes from ElevenLabs' own public materials. The *numbers* are assumptions, labelled as such in both files, and the routing logic is built so that swapping them changes nothing else. Every URL below was opened on 2026-09-20; nothing that failed to load is cited.

## Published frameworks

Three sources gave the policy its structure.

1. **Glencoyne, "SaaS Enterprise Discount Approval Matrix"** (October 2025), https://www.glencoyne.com/guides/enterprise-discount-approval-matrix. Contributed the band shape and the stacking of non-price triggers: a rep tier at 0 to 15 percent with net-30 and standard legal terms, a Head of Sales tier at 16 to 30 percent or net-60 or a term over 24 months, a CEO/Finance tier above 30 percent or net-90 or longer, and a separate Legal track for non-standard clauses; written justification above the rep tier; the matrix itself reviewed at least quarterly.
2. **DealHub, "What is Discount Approval?"** (glossary), https://dealhub.io/glossary/discount-approval/. Contributed tiered sign-off by discount size (its worked example is "a 10% discount needing sales manager approval, while a 25% discount requires the VP of Sales to sign off") and the deal desk's role as the gatekeeper that manages exceptions, enforces the pricing policy and documents every approval, which is the basis of §6 and §8.3.
3. **Ravenna, "Deal Desk Approval Workflow Without the Bottleneck"** (September 2026), https://ravenna.ai/blog/deal-desk-workflow-automation. Contributed approver stacking by trigger rather than by tier alone: non-standard payment terms go to Finance, legal exposure goes to Legal alongside the executive approver, strategic and regulated deals need Legal sign-off in addition to leadership, and a policy layer should "flag payment terms that deviate from standard net-30". That is the additive precedence rule in the YAML.

Three narrower points each rest on one source. Salesforce Trailhead's unit "Create an Approval Process" (https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/projects/build-a-discount-approval-process/create-an-approval-process) triggers approval on a discount-percentage criterion and resolves the approver from the opportunity owner's Manager field, which is the mechanical form of §8.1: the approver is derived from the requester's reporting line, never from the requester. Zluri's segregation-of-duties guide (https://www.zluri.com/blog/separation-of-duties-policy-example) names the exact conflict §8 forbids ("A sales operations administrator can both create custom pricing or discount approvals in the CRM and also approve their own deals under those custom terms") and resolves it by requiring sign-off from someone with no stake in the deal, which is §8.2. ORM Technologies' "How to Run a Deal Desk Process" (July 2026, https://orm-tech.com/blog/how-to-run-a-deal-desk-process) has exceptions expire ("An approved exception is valid for thirty days") and routes by trigger, not by deal size; the policy adopts expiry in a weaker, number-free form at §9.6 (approvals lapse when any business term changes and never carry to a later deal), because a day count would be one more threshold with no field behind it.

## First-party anchors from ElevenLabs

These are quoted, not modelled. The public price ladder at https://elevenlabs.io/pricing is Free $0, Starter $6, Creator $22, Pro $99, Scale $299, Business $990, Enterprise custom, and the same page says "Annual billing works out to two months free", a standing ten-for-twelve prepay allowance of about 16.7 percent. The agents page at https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/agents prices additional call minutes at $0.08 per minute, burst at $0.16 per minute and text messages at $0.003 each (the $0.080 and $0.160 figures also appear at https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api). Oriel's "list price" and its usage-based product lines are modelled on this ladder; the ladder is also why §1.2 treats the published annual allowance as a list price rather than a discount.

Seventeen Deployment Strategist listings (all 17 in the 235-listing Ashby corpus captured on 2026-09-20; one re-opened at https://elevenlabs.io/careers/8c068ebf-c79f-4f12-97ef-b4c9a4f7ae5f) use the same phrase: "structuring commercial terms (including outcome-based pricing, license fees, and implementation arrangements)". Those three terms are rules R6, R7 and R8 and their §2 definitions. The Deal Operations Strategist listing (https://elevenlabs.io/careers/7d01b1ec-511d-40bf-a6ed-88b212932edb) says "Work closely with engineering to maintain and evolve our Configure Price Quote tool" and "Develop and maintain pricing frameworks, discount guardrails, and approval policies that scale with the business", and names the approver parties: "aligning Finance, Legal, Product, and leadership so deals don't stall on process". Those four parties, plus the deal desk itself and the requesting Account Executive, are the six role keys in the YAML; "leadership" became the Chief Revenue Officer.

## Where the sources contradict or go silent

**No source gives ElevenLabs' thresholds.** There is no public approval matrix, margin floor, enterprise rate card or outcome-pricing mechanics; "true-up" and "clawback" appear zero times in 235 listings. Every number in the policy is therefore an assumption (table below), and the evaluator is written so that its correctness does not depend on them.

**Multi-year prepay.** The frameworks do not agree on what a long, prepaid term does to the discount band. Glencoyne moves any contract over 24 months up a tier, treating term as exposure, while saying in the same guide that multi-year commitments justify deeper upfront discounts. ElevenLabs' own ladder treats annual prepay as earned room (two months free). DealHub and Ravenna are silent. Whether a negotiated allowance sits inside the band or widens it is stated nowhere, so the policy does not state it either: §3.2 says only that such customers "have historically been granted additional room beyond the standard band" and that "Deal Desk maintains the schedule of allowances". That is R2. The YAML marks it `unresolved`, the evaluator adds Deal Desk and names the rule, and nothing guesses.

**The March memo.** R10 is fictional but the pattern is real: a memo (M-2026-03) raised the Deal Desk ceiling from 25 to 30 percent "for competitive renewals only", and the August rewrite (v4.2) printed 30 percent with no carve-out. Whether a new-logo or non-competitive deal at 25 to 30 percent still needs the Chief Revenue Officer is contested inside the document, and the document never resolves it. Glencoyne's quarterly matrix review and ORM's thirty-day expiry exist precisely because memos and the policies they amend drift apart. The evaluator treats R10 as unresolved and escalates to Deal Desk by name.

## Assumption table

| Threshold | Where it appears | Why this value | Swappable via (`policy/pricing_policy.yaml`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 percent (Account Executive ceiling) | §3.1.1, R1a and R1b boundary | Glencoyne's rep tier is 0 to 15 percent; it also sits just under ElevenLabs' standing 16.7 percent annual allowance, so an AE cannot give away more than the public price page already does | `R1a` `lte: 1500` and `R1b` `gt: 1500` (basis points) |
| 30 percent (Deal Desk ceiling, CRO floor) | §3.1.2 and §3.1.3, R1b and R1c boundary | Top of Glencoyne's second tier; DealHub's example escalates to a VP at 25, so 30 is the generous end of published practice | `R1b` `lte: 3000` and `R1c` `gt: 3000` |
| 25 percent (pre-memo ceiling, lower edge of the contested band) | §10 memo line, R10 | DealHub's VP threshold; chosen so the contested band (25 to 30) is narrow and realistic | `R10` `gt: 2500` and the §10 memo line in the prose |
| 24 months (multi-year) | §3.2, R2 | Glencoyne treats a term over 24 months as a tier change; two years is the usual floor for "multi-year" | `R2` `term_months gte: 24` |
| net-30 (standard payment terms) | §4.1, R3 | Glencoyne's rep-tier baseline; Ravenna's "standard net-30"; anything longer routes to Finance | `R3` `payment_terms in: [net_45, net_60, net_90, other]`, enum in `policy/schema.py` |
| $1,000,000 net total (CRO) | §7.1, R11 | ElevenLabs' enterprise AE listings describe "six-figure deal sizes" and its strategic AE listings "seven-figure+ deals"; the CRO line is drawn where seven figures begin | `R11` `net_total_cents gte: 100000000` |

## Citation traps avoided

- A passage about a "modern GTM technology stack... CPQ" that search engines attribute to ElevenLabs is from a Postman job posting that co-ranked with theirs. Not cited.
- `chargebee.com/pricing-repository/elevenlabs` is a pricing teardown in Chargebee's public pricing repository, not a customer case study. m3ter's "Top 20 best AI pricing pages" and its AI-pricing series use ElevenLabs as an example pricing page, not as a customer. `revpath.dealhub.io` is a hosted job board on a DealHub subdomain; an ElevenLabs listing appearing there is syndication, not evidence of DealHub as ElevenLabs' CPQ. None are cited.
- Salesforce Help's CPQ Advanced Approvals and Approval Chains articles, the Advanced Approvals PDF and the Trailhead project landing page returned only an application shell or a certificate error when fetched, so the canonical vendor docs are not cited; the one Trailhead unit that rendered is. Salesforce's investment in ElevenLabs and its role as their sales system of record are not evidence of Salesforce CPQ, which the Help site now marks as receiving no new feature development.
- Third-party pricing round-ups (Quiq, Cekura, Flexprice and similar) repeat the ElevenLabs figures; the first-party pages are cited instead.
